01 — The problem

Today's feeds are built to keep you scrolling.

Big platforms collect huge amounts of personal data to decide what to show you next. The goal isn't to serve you. It's to hold your attention for as long as possible.

  • Doomscrolling

    Recommendation systems are designed to maximise time on the app, with a real cost to mental health.

  • Echo chambers

    Engagement-ranked feeds tend to push divisive and extreme content, because it keeps people reacting.

  • Over-collection of data

    Your behaviour is tracked, profiled and sold to advertisers, often in ways you never agreed to or noticed.

02 — How it works

A simpler platform, on purpose.

H#SHTAG goes back to the basics of early social media: text posts and images, organised by people and topics rather than by a hidden ranking system.

  1. Follow people and hashtags Your feed contains only accounts and topics you've chosen. If you didn't follow it, you won't see it.
  2. Posts appear in order No ranking, no boosting, no "suggested for you". Just what's new from your follows.
  3. Ads are tied to topics, not to you Advertisers pick hashtags, like #tech, instead of targeting by age, gender or browsing history.
  4. Your data stays yours We only process what's needed to run the platform, and we never sell it.
03 — Why now

Regulation is moving in this direction.

Australia is planning to let people switch off social media algorithms, and has a record of being copied by the UK and EU on social media rules. As those laws arrive, the major platforms will be scrambling to retrofit a non-algorithmic option.

H#SHTAG is built that way from the first line of code.

  • Australia

    Planned law would let people switch off social media algorithms.

  • United Kingdom

    Has already followed Australia's lead with its under-16 social media rules.

  • European Union

    Moving the same way, so we expect similar rules on algorithms to follow.

04 — Who it's for

Starting on campus.

Facebook began inside universities, and so will we. Our first users are 18–34s in the UK, Ireland, Australia and the EU: students, creators and hobbyists who want a calmer, more transparent place to talk, and who belong to communities that are easy to reach.
